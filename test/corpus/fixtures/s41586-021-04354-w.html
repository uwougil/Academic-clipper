Abstract

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are millisecond-duration extragalactic radio pulses. We localize repeating FRB 20200120E with milliarcsecond precision to a globular cluster associated with the nearby galaxy M81, ruling out young magnetar models formed by core-collapse supernovae.

Main

Milliarcsecond localization was achieved via the European VLBI Network (EVN) observed at central frequency \(\nu_0 = 1.4\ \mathrm{GHz}\) with burst fluence \(F_{\nu} > 0.1\ \mathrm{Jy\ ms}\) 1,2. As mapped in Figure 1, the burst coordinates coincide with the core of globular cluster M81-GC.

The isotropic-equivalent radio burst energy \(E_{\nu}\) obeys Equation (1) at distance \(D = 3.63 \pm 0.05\ \mathrm{Mpc}\):

$$E_{\nu} = \frac{4 \pi D^2 F_{\nu}}{(1 + z)}$$
(1)

Peak burst luminosity reaches \(L_{\mathrm{peak}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{38}\ \mathrm{erg\ s^{-1}}\). Dispersion measure (DM) corrections in Equation (2) isolate the Milky Way halo contribution:

$$\mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{host}} = \mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{obs}} - \mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{MW}} - \mathrm{DM}_{\mathrm{halo}}$$
(2)

Burst dynamic spectra and polarization angles are portrayed in Figure 2 and tabulated in Table 1.

As verified against Figure 3, Extended Data Fig. 1, and Extended Data Fig. 2, the absence of an underlying persistent radio source constrains companion models.

Methods

Baseband data were recorded at 1.4 GHz and correlated with the SFXC software correlator.
