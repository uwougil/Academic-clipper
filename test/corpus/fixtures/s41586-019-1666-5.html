Abstract

The promise of quantum computers is that certain computational tasks might be executed exponentially faster on a quantum processor than on a classical computer. We demonstrate computational supremacy with a 53-qubit programmable superconducting processor executing random quantum circuits.

Main

Our Sycamore processor comprises 54 transmon qubits arranged in a two-dimensional grid 1,2. The computational state space of dimension \(2^{53} \approx 9 \times 10^{15}\) is explored via single-qubit gates \(U_i \in \mathrm{SU}(2)\) with entangling phase \(\theta = \pi/2\) and low average gate error \(e_g < 10^{-3}\) as outlined in Figure 1.

$$\vert \psi_U \rangle = U \vert 0^{\otimes n} \rangle = U_m \dots U_1 \vert 0^{\otimes n} \rangle$$
(1)

The ideal probability distribution of bitstrings \(x \in \{0, 1\}^n\) satisfies Equation (1) with probability \(P_U(x) = \vert \langle x \vert \psi_U \rangle \vert^2\). To benchmark circuit performance, the linear cross-entropy benchmarking fidelity \(F_{\mathrm{XEB}}\) is formulated as:

$$F_{\mathrm{XEB}} = 2^n \sum_{x} P_U(x) q(x) - 1$$
(2)

For an incoherent noise model represented in Equation (3), the density matrix \(\rho\) decays exponentially:

$$\rho = F_{\mathrm{XEB}} \vert \psi_U \rangle \langle \psi_U \vert + (1 - F_{\mathrm{XEB}}) \frac{I}{2^n}$$
(3)

Furthermore, error budget summation follows the array formulation in Equation (4):

$$\begin{array}{c} \epsilon_{\mathrm{tot}} = \prod_{j} (1 - e_j) \\ F_{\mathrm{pred}} \approx \exp\left( - \sum_j e_j \right) \end{array}$$
(4)
