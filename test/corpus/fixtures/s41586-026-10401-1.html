Abstract

Magnetism has seen substantial progress in recent decades. Here we present a classification of magnetic order using the spin space group (SSG) theory. On the basis of whether net spin magnetization is constrained to zero, we systematically categorize magnetic orders into ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism. We introduce oriented spin space groups (OSSGs) such as \({P}^{-1}{6}_{3}/{m}^{1}\) and analyze spin-space point groups \(P_{\mathrm{spin}}\) with magnetization \(M_{s}\) and temperature scales \(T_{\mathrm{c}}^{0}\).

Main

The dichotomy between ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism is rooted in net spin magnetization 1,2,3,4. As observed in Figure 1 and Table 1, magnetic sublattices can be crystallographically equivalent.

$$H = \sum_{\mathbf{k}} \psi^\dagger_{\mathbf{k}} \left( \epsilon_{\mathbf{k}} \tau_z + \mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{k}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} \right) \psi_{\mathbf{k}}$$
(1)

The parameter \(\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{k}}\) satisfies the relation in Equation (2):

$$\Delta(\mathbf{k}) = i (\mathbf{d}_{\mathbf{k}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}) \sigma_y$$
(2)

Further structural confirmations are provided in Extended Data Fig. 1 and Extended Data Fig. 2, as well as supplementary properties in Table 2.
